A research brief is the one page that keeps a study honest. It names what you're trying to find out, who you need to hear from, and what decision the findings feed. Write it before you recruit anyone — if you can't fill it in yet, the study isn't ready to run.

## What a brief covers

The skill writes a full plan: background, stakeholders, hypotheses, goals, success criteria, methodology, participants, deliverables, and a timeline. It also forces a distinction people skip. "Signups are down" is a business problem, not a user problem, and the brief won't let the two get merged into one goal.

## Add it to Claude, no code needed

You don't need Claude Code or a repo to use this. [Download the skill](/research-brief.skill), then in Claude (web, desktop, or mobile) open Settings, go to Capabilities, and upload it under Skills. It works in any chat from then on, the same as the Claude Code version.

## Using the skill

Bring it whatever you have: a Slack message, a half-formed hunch, a stakeholder request. "We want to know why teachers stop using Glow after week two" is enough to start from.

Say "help me plan this study" or "I need a research brief" and it takes over from there. It asks for what's missing rather than guessing at an objective or a participant count.
