---
title: Standards
description: The must layer. Checkable rules that bind every screen we ship, human-built or agent-built.
---

The guidance pages tell you how to decide and what usually works. The catalog is different: every entry in it is a **standard**, and a standard is a requirement. If a screen violates one, it doesn't ship, no matter who or what built it.

Each standard is one verifiable statement with explicit fail conditions. "Text meets WCAG AA contrast" is a standard. "Interfaces should feel calm" is not; that's guidance, because no machine can check it. Checkability is what makes this work: it lets the bar be enforced automatically, before anyone sits down to review.

The same catalog binds both audiences:

- **Humans** read it rendered in the [catalog](/standards/catalog), and cite IDs in reviews ("this fails SLP-3").
- **AI agents** read the identical catalog as [YAML](/standards/catalog.yaml) and apply it while generating UI.

Standards come in three tiers:

- **L0 — non-negotiable.** Accessibility floors, destructive-action safety. No waiver exists; a violation blocks ship.
- **L1 — mandatory.** Binding by default. Deviation requires a documented waiver in code: `dx-waive <ID> reason="<specific reason>"`.
- **L2 — recommended.** Follow unless you have a better answer; deviating needs an inline rationale.
