Most of this site tells you what the harness does. This page tells you what we
were thinking when we built it, and what we still argue about.

## Where we think this stands

Before we settled on this shape, we studied the harnesses we respect and
measured ours against them. With today's models, we think what we built holds
up. We are proud of that.

We are also careful with the word "today". Models improve every few months, and
each improvement moves the line between what a harness must supply and what a
model brings on its own. Some of the scaffolding we built this year will be
unnecessary next year. We would rather write that down now than let this site
imply we found the answer. We found an answer that works now, and we keep
checking whether it still does.

## Why DESIGN.md lives in your repo

We could have kept every product's design language in one central place. We
chose the opposite: a skill that interviews you, reads your code, and writes a
`DESIGN.md` that stays in your product's repo.

We chose it because your context is not our context. Every team ships under
different constraints: a different audience, a different codebase, a different
history of decisions that made sense at the time. A central file would flatten
those differences or go stale trying to track them. A file in your repo evolves
the way the rest of your product does — through pull requests, by the people
closest to the work.

It also keeps the choice of design system yours. If your team already has one,
`DESIGN.md` points at it. If you want a different one, adopt it without asking
us. The control catalog still holds the floors that protect users: contrast,
target sizes, copy that says what happens. Everything above those floors
belongs to your team.

That is the trade we are trying to make: consistent where inconsistency would
hurt users, flexible where your product knows better than we do.

## Not set in stone

We built the structure to hold more than it holds today. The catalog can carry
another design system's rules. The skills are a set we curate, and we expect to
add some and retire others. If a better way of working with agents appears, the
harness should be able to absorb it — and if it can't, we will discard the
harness before we discard the better way.

That sentence is easy to write and hard to mean, so here is what it costs us:
re-reading our own decisions when a new model ships, retiring work we were fond
of, and telling you when something here is out of date instead of quietly
leaving it up. What stays constant is the job underneath: adjusting how we
support teams working with AI, and keeping that support within reach of people
who do not write code every day.

If this page stops being true, the fix is to change the harness, not the page.

The TransformX product design team
