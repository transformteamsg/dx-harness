# Output format

Lay the plan out in this shape. Keep every heading. Under `UX METRICS`, a generative
study writes `Not applicable: generative study`.

```
RESEARCH PLAN: [Project name]
Researcher: [Name]
Date: [Date]
Version: [1.0]

PROJECT BACKGROUND
[Two to four sentences on context, the business problem, the user problem, and the decisions this research will inform]

INTERNAL STAKEHOLDERS
Responsible: [Name, role]
Accountable: [Name, role]
Consulted: [Name, role]
Informed: [Name, role]

HYPOTHESES
- [Hypothesis 1]

ASSUMPTIONS
- [Assumption 1]

RESEARCH STATEMENT
[One or two sentences that use the research statement model]

RESEARCH GOALS
1. [Goal 1]
2. [Goal 2]
3. [Goal 3, if needed]

SUCCESS CRITERIA
- [Criterion 1]
- [Criterion 2]

METHODOLOGY
[Method or methods, and a brief rationale]

UX METRICS
- [Metric 1]
- [Metric 2]

PARTICIPANTS AND RECRUITMENT
Criteria: [List]
Number: [X participants, Y per segment]

Session format: [Remote or in person]
Session length: [X minutes]

Screener: [Link]

DELIVERABLES AND NEXT STEPS

- [Deliverable 1, and why this format]

[Included here, or a link to a separate document]

RESOURCES

- Screener survey: [link]
- Synthesis board: [link]
- Report: [link]
```

## Researcher reminders

End the plan with these reminders:

- Do not write interview questions before the stakeholders agree on the research goals. Each question serves a goal.
- A business problem is not a user problem. Name both, and make sure the goals address the user problem.
- Set three research goals at most for a study.
- Over-recruit by at least 20%.
- Update the plan as the project changes, especially the resources section.
- Do not start the project until the stakeholders have seen the plan. Align before you recruit.
- Where a stakeholder struggles to say what they need, use the fill-in sentence: "I need [information] to understand [goal] to make [decision] that will impact [organisational goal]."
