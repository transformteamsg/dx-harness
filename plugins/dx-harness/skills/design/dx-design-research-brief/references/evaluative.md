# Evaluative study

Guidance for an evaluative study, and for the evaluative phase of a hybrid study. A
hybrid study runs the generative phase first, then an evaluative follow-up.

**Research goals** (component 4). Common evaluative goals:

- Evaluate how people use or interact with [product, website, app, or service]
- Uncover the tools people use to [achieve goal] and their experience with those tools
- Uncover the pain points or confusion people meet when they interact with [prototype or experience]

**Methodology** (component 6):

| Phase | Methods |
| --- | --- |
| Qualitative | Qualitative usability test or experience feedback session, concept testing |
| Quantitative | Quantitative usability test, tree testing, first-click testing, surveys with metrics |

Use qualitative to learn why users behave a certain way, or to test a mid-fidelity
design. Use quantitative to learn how many and how fast, or to benchmark a live product.

**UX metrics** (component 7). For a quantitative phase, list the metrics you will track:

- Time on task
- Task success (pass or fail, or a three-point scale)
- Number of errors
- SEQ (Single Ease Question)
- UMUX or SUS
- Other, such as SMEQ or confidence

Where no phase is quantitative, write `Not applicable: qualitative study`.

**Participants** (component 8):

| Study | Participants |
| --- | --- |
| Qualitative usability test | Seven to 10 per segment |
| Quantitative usability test | Five per segment at minimum when moderated, 15 or more when unmoderated |
| Benchmarking | 25 or more per segment |

**Timeline** (component 10): two to four weeks.

**Hybrid timeline** (component 10): three to five weeks for both phases together. Use
this duration, not the generative or evaluative one.
