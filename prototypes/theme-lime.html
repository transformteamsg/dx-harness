2 — the steps, straight off the Radix scales

Nothing here is hand-mixed. Base is lime-9 #BDEE63, the value confirmed in the DXD Figma. Hover takes the mode's own step 10; pressed takes step 10 from the other direction, so the fill goes brighter under the cursor and darker under the finger. Radix builds lime as a "bright" scale whose steps 9 and 10 are designed to carry dark text rather than white — the same split this theme needs from the other side. White on lime-9 is 1.35:1 and is never used.

3 — which black

All of them carry lime-9 at 13:1 or better, so this is not a contrast decision. Pure #000 leaves nowhere to go down — insets, wells and the scrim under a dialog all have to be faked with a border — and at 15.6:1 the lime edges bloom on OLED at display sizes. #060707 reads as black, keeps a four-step ladder above it, and still allows a darker inset if one is ever needed.

Notes — the decisions behind the numbers

  1. Lime is a text colour on dark and a fill colour on light. It never swaps. At 15.0:1 on #060707, lime-9 carries body text, links and code keywords in the dark world. On paper the same value drops to 1.3:1 and carries nothing, so the light world hands text to lime-11 and keeps lime-9 for fills only. Radix designs lime as a "bright" scale whose steps 9 and 10 take dark text rather than white, which is the same split from the other side.
  2. Dark ink on lime, never white. White on lime-9 is 1.35:1 — unusable. Every lime fill takes --lime-ink, and the hover and pressed steps were chosen so the ink still clears AAA on them in the dark world (12.7:1 at the darkest).
  3. Hover and pressed both come from step 10, in opposite directions. Hover uses the mode's own step 10 (dark #D4FF70, light #B0E64C); pressed uses step 10 from the other side. No hand-mixing, and the direction reverses correctly on light.
  4. The re-derivation fixed the old worst pair on dark. The previous hand-mixed --lime-dim sat at 4.5:1 and did two jobs badly. Radix lime-8 #577538 is 3.9:1 on the background — it clears the 3:1 UI floor on its own, so --lime-line now covers borders and meaningful strokes with one value. The dark column has no failing pair. Its worst is --lime-line at 3.9:1, and that is non-text.
  5. What the re-derivation broke: light-mode lime text. Radix calibrates step 11 against its own scale's steps 1–2, not against a warm neutral or a tinted step 3. On --muted and --lime-wash, lime-11 lands at 4.25:1 and 4.29:1 — both fail. The earlier hand-mixed #4C6B1F passed everywhere. The fix, when light is unparked, is to step text on tinted fills down to lime-12 #37401C (9.7:1 and 9.8:1). Left failing and marked rather than papered over, because light is parked.
  6. Light also loses the teal dot. Teal-9 is 2.9:1 on warm paper and misses the 3:1 floor for a solid indicator; teal-10 or teal-11 is needed there. Teal-11 itself is 4.36:1 on paper and fails as text, so the light --success is teal-12. Same root cause as note 5; both resolve together in the docs retheme.
  7. Success is teal so a verdict never reads as brand. Grass-9 #46A758 and lime-9 are the same family — a green pass badge beside a lime brand mark stops meaning "pass". Teal-11 on teal-3 is 8.1:1 on dark and sits far enough round the wheel that the badge reads as a verdict, not as decoration. The verdict row under the cards shows the L0 chip, the L1 chip and the pass badge together.
  8. Near-black, not pure black. #060707 over #000: it reads identically as "black", keeps a four-step ladder above it for surface, muted and hover fills, and leaves a darker step available for wells and scrims. Linear reserves #010102 for marketing pages only and runs its product on #08090a; this sits between.
  9. Elevation is surface steps and hairlines. No glows, no lime bloom behind the hero — the dark world separates by the 1.05/1.08 surface ladder and the --border hairline, exactly as the current .landing-dark block does.
